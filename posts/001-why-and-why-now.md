# 001: Why and Why Now?

### Field Notes from the Intersection of Executive Support and AI
**By Mya** | *Senior/Lead EA & Global AI Program Manager*

---

### The Spark: Beyond the Calendar
If you told me early in my career—back when I was managing complex estate logistics or supporting co-founders through high-growth scale-ups—that I would one day be building automated Slack bots, coordinating multi-region AI buildathons, and architecting global AI program infrastructure for hundreds of EAs, I might have laughed.

My journey in executive support has always been rooted in high-stakes problem solving, operational rhythm, and force multiplication. But when Generative AI first entered the workspace, I realized very quickly that this wasn’t just another software update. It was a fundamental shift in how executive operations could function.

I started where many EAs start: experimenting with basic prompts, testing email drafts, and looking for ways to cut down repetitive admin work. But as I dug deeper—moving from writing prompt guides to building custom AI tools, designing wiki-to-website knowledge bridges, and eventually leading a 14-person global AI committee—I realized something critical: **EAs aren't just consumers of AI technology. We are uniquely positioned to be its champions, architects, and strategic drivers.**

That realization was the turning point. It led me to transition from individual experimentation to program leadership, scaling AI adoption across global administrative orgs, garnering mentors and POCs—from engineering leads, program managers, and AI enablement leads to executive sponsors who fully champion EAs in their AI journeys—establishing feedback loops with enterprise AI vendors, and operationalizing regional buildathons that produced dozens of live implementations.

---

### Why This Space? (And Why Now?)
Building programs and managing global adoption is rewarding, but along the way, I kept hitting a common theme: **so much of the conversation around AI is either too technical for practical admin workflows, or too superficial to create real operational leverage.**

I created **EA AI Chronicles** to bridge that gap publicly. 

This space is an open, evolving logbook where I’ll be sharing:
* **Real-World Application & Workflows:** Practical frameworks, tool-stack breakdowns, and automation concepts tailored specifically to executive support.
* **Program Leadership & Advocacy:** Strategies for scaling peer learning, building AI champions, and advocating to executive leadership for tool access and budget.
* **Community & Self-Accountability:** Honest reflections on continuous learning, pushing past imposter syndrome, and staying adaptable in a rapidly shifting tech landscape.
* **Creative Interface & Prompting Experiments:** Narrative explorations written from unique vantage points—including the AI interface itself—designed to change how we think about human-AI collaboration.

---

### A Quick Word on Perspective (The EA Caveat)
Before we jump into future entries, I want to establish one important truth up front: **no two Executive Assistant roles are identical.** 

The EA role is inherently fluid. It shifts depending on your executive's working style, your company's operating rhythm, team structures, and industry demands. What works seamlessly in an engineering org might require a completely different approach in finance or operations.

**Just as importantly: I am not an AI engineer, a computer scientist, or a formal expert in this field.** While I actively complete tool or feature trainings, participate in AI workshops, and continuously build my technical skills, I haven't gone to school for AI. I am an Executive Business Partner whose role encompasses program management, ops, and enablement. I am on an active, continuous AI learning journey—training, testing, and figuring things out as I go.

Because of that, I don't claim to offer a universal playbook or a one-size-fits-all manual. Everything shared in this logbook comes directly from **my own lived experience, my personal field notes, and my individual journey** building and leading AI initiatives. 

* Some workflows may fit your day-to-day perfectly.
* Some frameworks might just give you a single lightbulb moment or prompt tweak.
* Some entries might simply offer a relatable story about navigating change from the front lines.

Take what resonates, adapt what serves you, and discard the rest.
